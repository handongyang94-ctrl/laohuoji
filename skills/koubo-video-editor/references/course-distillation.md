# Course Distillation

This file condenses the editing course into reusable rules for this skill. It is not a note dump. It is the editorial judgment layer.

## Core Editing Model

The course treats editing as a decision system, not software operation. A finished cut is built from six layers:

1. Content logic: what information or emotion must arrive, and in what order.
2. Visual logic: how shots connect by movement, focus, color, shot size, shape, direction, or action.
3. Rhythm logic: where the viewer needs speed, pause, impact, or breathing space.
4. Audio logic: how speech, music, ambient sound, and sound bridges pull the viewer through cuts.
5. Proof logic: when the viewer must see product, process, result, place, sample, or action instead of only hearing a claim.
6. Delivery logic: whether the output is rough cut, packaged oral version, preview, or finished publishable video.

For Codex, this means: do not start from effects. Start from why the next shot should exist.

## Finished Video vs Stage Output

The course examples do not stop at "speech is cut and subtitles exist". They move through music, section rhythm, B-roll/proof shots, transitions, detail choices, and final audio balance.

Use these labels:

- `raw rough cut`: selected takes, unfinished joins.
- `stage-1 oral lock`: meaning and cut points are locked, no final packaging.
- `packaged oral version`: captions and simple overlays are added, but B-roll/music may still be absent.
- `finished short video`: meaning, cut points, B-roll/visual plan, music/sound, captions, layout, render, and final inspection all pass.

Never present a packaged oral version as finished when the user asked for 成片.

## Material First

The course repeatedly says that many weak edits happen because the editor has not eaten the material.

Before cutting:

- browse the footage
- know what happens in the material
- classify shots by content and function
- understand the script/voiceover
- divide the video into sections
- imagine what picture should appear for each line

Agent equivalent:

- create an inventory
- generate contact sheets for large folders
- run ASR when speech matters
- map script meaning units to source footage
- mark missing visual proof

## Lens and Shot Language

Use the course's lens-language material as a tagging system for source footage:

- Shot size: extreme wide, wide, full, medium, close, detail.
- Angle: front, side, back, overhead, low angle, high angle.
- Movement: static, push, pull, pan, tilt, tracking, handheld, orbit, zoom.
- Composition/focus: where the viewer's eye lands first.
- Subject/action: what changes inside the shot.
- Emotional function: clean, tense, premium, warm, mysterious, fast, chaotic, trustworthy.

When cutting, keep the viewer's visual focus controllable. Fast cutting is allowed only when focus remains legible.

## Continuity and Invisible Editing

Use invisible editing when the goal is smoothness, premium feel, product display, travel beauty, or credible story continuity.

Main rules:

- Motion to motion: connect moving shots when movement direction and speed are compatible.
- Static to static: connect static shots when composition and focus do not jump violently.
- Motion to static: use a movement slowdown, internal subject movement, or matched visual force to bridge.
- Match internal direction: if a hand, person, object, or vehicle moves left-to-right, prefer the next shot to continue or answer that direction.
- Visual focus continuity: avoid forcing the eye to jump left-right-top-bottom without purpose.
- Tone continuity: avoid alternating bright/dark or cold/warm shots unless contrast is intentional.
- Similarity cuts: connect by similar color, shape, action, person position, shot size, or camera motion.
- Action continuity: continue the same action across different scenes to hide the cut.
- J-cut: let the next sound enter before the next picture.
- L-cut: let the previous sound continue after the picture changes.
- Mask/occlusion transition: use passing objects, body movement, frame wipes, doorways, foreground shapes, or dark frames only when the source material naturally supports it.

Automation rule: invisible edits need metadata. Tag movement direction, shot size, focus region, and tone before choosing transitions.

## Jump Editing and Fast Rhythm

Use visible/jump editing when the goal is energy, ad impact, card-point rhythm, montage, short-form retention, or deliberate style.

Main rules:

- Cut on music beats only when the image also has a reason to change.
- Avoid pure beat-slavery: a beat is a chance to cut, not an obligation.
- Strong jumps need one stabilizer: same action, same direction, same color, same object, same composition, or same semantic category.
- Faster rhythm tolerates larger visual jumps, but the viewer still needs a readable subject.
- Use detail shots to accelerate without confusing the viewer.
- Use repeated shot-size patterns to create rhythm: wide-detail-medium-detail, face-object-face-evidence, action-result-action-result.
- Use speed ramps only when they clarify movement or intensify a beat.
- Give heavy judgments, reveals, price points, and emotional turns a small landing beat.

Automation rule: a fast edit plan should still explain the connection between adjacent shots.

## Action Cutting

The course's practical action rule: most everyday actions have start, middle, and end. If the full action is not meaningful, keep the useful middle.

Use this for:

- grabbing keys
- picking up product
- opening a door
- walking into frame
- operating a machine
- showing a sample
- parking, starting, locking, handing over, placing, pouring

Rules:

- remove preparation when it does not add meaning
- keep the clear action moment
- remove dead ending after the result is clear
- keep full action only when the process itself is proof
- use close/detail shots to make action feel clean

## Music and Sound

Music is not background wallpaper. It sets emotion, speed, segmentation, and expectation.

Use music by role:

- Drive: fast ad, montage, product rhythm, exercise, fashion.
- Atmosphere: travel, food, store scene, emotional memory.
- Trust: low-volume, low-distraction bed under talking-head speech.
- Contrast: warm/cold, calm/impact, before/after.
- Structure: intro rise, body pulse, climax, ending hit.

Course-derived music rules for automation:

- Find music after understanding material and project background.
- Use project type and source material to narrow keywords; do not search blindly.
- For speech-led pieces, prefer clean instrumental music and duck under speech.
- For multi-section pieces, music can mark section changes and emotional phases.
- Listen for music structure before cutting music-led visuals: intro, main groove, lift, climax, release, ending.
- Avoid stiff beat slavery. Feel with the music instead of cutting every beat mechanically.

Use sound by edit function:

- J-cut to pull the next scene in.
- L-cut to smooth a visual jump.
- Ambient sound to make generated or static assets feel less dead.
- SFX only on meaningful impacts, reveals, swipes, UI/text hits, product actions, or transitions.
- Duck music under important speech.

For talking-head work, speech intelligibility wins over music energy.

## Narrative and Logic

The logic lessons matter even for non-story videos. A video can be organized by:

- Time line: before, during, after.
- Space line: exterior, entrance, interior, detail, people, result.
- Action line: preparation, operation, result.
- Object line: product exterior, feature, use, detail, outcome.
- Emotion line: calm, curiosity, tension, release.
- Problem-solution line: wrong action, consequence, correction, result.
- Contrast line: old/new, bad/good, cheap/expensive, ordinary/premium.
- Character line: person, goal, obstacle, choice, result.

When source footage feels scattered, choose one primary line and let every shot answer it.

## B-Roll and Proof Logic

B-roll should do a job:

- prove a spoken claim
- show the thing being discussed
- show process or result
- compress an abstract idea
- bridge a jump
- reset attention
- carry a music-led section
- hide an unavoidable A-roll cut

For talking-head business/knowledge videos:

- keep face for stance, warning, conclusion, and trust
- use B-roll for product, sample, process, scene, cost, result, and evidence
- use text cards for numbers, comparisons, lists, warnings
- use generated images only when source footage cannot provide the visual job

A finished video requires a B-roll decision pass even if the final choice is face-only.

## Project-Type Routes

### Talking-Head / Knowledge / Trust

Priority: meaning lock, take selection, cut-point cleanup, captions, selective B-roll, music/silence decision.

Do not overdecorate. The face carries trust. Use B-roll to show evidence, compress abstract points, reset attention, or make a concrete claim visible.

### Product / Commercial

Priority: product feature logic, detail shots, premium continuity, music rhythm, color/tone consistency.

Use object-line editing:

1. whole product or problem scene
2. use scene
3. detail/feature
4. result/proof
5. brand/action

### Fast Ad

Priority: hook, beat, contrast, hard information density, readable subject.

Use jump editing, details, text emphasis, impact SFX, and short segments. The viewer must still know what they are looking at.

### Travel / Visual Short

Priority: atmosphere, movement continuity, spatial line, color/tone, visual focus.

Use invisible editing or tasteful jump rhythm. Avoid random scenic fragments.

### Story / Micro-Film

Priority: character intention, conflict, emotional continuity, reaction shots, sound bridge.

Do not cut away reaction beats merely to save time. Story comprehension depends on reaction and causality.

### Teaching / Knowledge

Priority: clear logic, spoken credibility, caption hierarchy, examples, visual compression.

Use text cards only when they reduce cognitive load. Do not turn the video into a slide deck.

## What Transfers Into Automation

Convert course ideas into these machine-readable fields:

- `meaning_unit`: semantic unit that must survive the edit
- `intent`: hook/setup/judgment/evidence/contrast/consequence/action/CTA
- `shot_size`: wide/medium/close/detail
- `movement`: static/push/pull/pan/tilt/tracking/handheld/zoom
- `direction`: left-right/right-left/up/down/toward/away/none
- `focus_region`: left/center/right/top/bottom/product/face/text
- `tone`: bright/dark/neutral, warm/cold/neutral
- `visual_job`: evidence/detail/bridge/reset/emotion/product/action/text/face-only
- `rhythm_weight`: light/normal/heavy
- `cut_style`: invisible/jump/match/action/j-cut/l-cut/mask/fade
- `audio_role`: speech/music/ambient/sfx/bridge/silence
- `proof_need`: none/source/generate/text
- `gate_status`: missing/planned/implemented/verified

If these fields are absent, infer them and report uncertainty rather than silently guessing.
