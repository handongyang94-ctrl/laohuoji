# Editor Decision System

This is the course-distilled operating system for the skill. It converts the lessons into repeatable editorial behavior.

## 1. Editing Starts Before Cutting

The course repeats this across product, travel, ad, story, and short-video lessons: first understand the project, the material, and the script. Bad edits usually start because the editor cuts before knowing what the footage is supposed to do.

Before touching the timeline, answer:

- What is the video type: talking-head, product, ad, travel, story, teaching, account content, mixed?
- What is the primary line: time, space, action, object, emotion, problem-solution, contrast, character?
- What is the strongest proof source: face, product, place, action, result, screenshot, generated visual?
- Which sections are speech-led and which are music-led?
- What is the viewer supposed to understand in the first 2 seconds and trust by 5 seconds?

If these are unclear, create a provisional edit plan instead of rendering.

## 2. The Five-Layer Cut

Every cut must be justified by at least one layer:

1. Information: a new meaning unit begins, ends, contrasts, or proves something.
2. Action: a visible action reaches its useful middle, result, or turn.
3. Visual focus: the viewer's eye can move naturally from one focus point to the next.
4. Rhythm: the music, speech, or emotional pacing needs a change.
5. Sound: J-cut, L-cut, ambience, impact, or music transition pulls the next image in.

Decorative cuts are allowed only when the video type is explicitly style-led and the subject remains readable.

## 3. Material Digestion

The course treats material familiarity as the first cause of good editing. Build a material map:

- `A-roll`: direct speech, host, interview, narration, dialogue.
- `B-roll proof`: product, process, place, result, evidence.
- `B-roll atmosphere`: environment, texture, movement, mood.
- `B-roll bridge`: transition shots, occlusion, empty frames, color/focus bridges.
- `Action sets`: start, middle, end of a movement.
- `Dead material`: setup, shaky ends, accidental blocking, duplicate failed attempts.

Do not use B-roll only because it exists. Do not skip B-roll because the face is available.

## 4. Project Routes

### Talking-Head / Knowledge / Trust

Purpose: clarity and credibility.

Default structure:

- face for judgment, warning, cost, conclusion
- B-roll for proof, product, process, evidence, example, visual reset
- text card for numbers, comparison, checklist, contrast
- low-volume music bed or deliberate silence

Cut style:

- tight sentence joins
- preserve judgment pauses
- no raw shooting breaths
- no random decorative transitions
- B-roll should not hide weak meaning; it should prove or compress meaning

Hard failure: calling a captioned face-only video a finished video when the task asked for a complete edit.

### Product / Commercial

Purpose: make the product legible, desirable, and believable.

Use object-line editing:

1. whole object or problem scene
2. use scenario
3. feature/detail
4. result/proof
5. brand/action/CTA

Choose music from product positioning: clean business, premium, fast energy, warm lifestyle, technical trust. Do not use fake luxury if the source is real and practical.

### Fast Ad / Short Promotional Cut

Purpose: impact and retention.

Use:

- hard hook
- quick detail flashes
- contrast
- beat-aware edits
- readable subject every shot
- strong text hierarchy

Cut fast, but never make the viewer search for the subject.

### Travel / Visual Short

Purpose: atmosphere, space, and flow.

Use:

- spatial line: exterior -> route -> detail -> people -> result
- color/tone continuity
- movement-direction continuity
- music section structure
- scenic shots long enough to breathe

### Story / Micro-Film

Purpose: causality and emotion.

Use:

- character goal
- obstacle
- choice
- reaction
- consequence
- emotional landing

Do not delete reaction beats just to shorten.

## 5. Action Cutting

For action footage, cut the useful middle:

- remove preparation when it is not meaningful
- keep the action moment that tells the story
- remove dead ending after the result is clear
- for repeated actions, vary shot size or angle

Example rule from the course: when someone grabs clothes/keys/vehicle controls, the full start-middle-end is often unnecessary. Use the middle action to make the sequence clean and light.

## 6. Continuity and Flow

Invisible smoothness uses:

- motion-to-motion
- static-to-static
- moving shot settling before static shot
- internal action matching
- focus-region continuity
- color/brightness continuity
- left-exit/right-entry or right-exit/left-entry for character movement
- similar shapes, colors, or shot sizes

If the cut feels jumpy, find at least one stabilizer: same action, same direction, same focus area, same color family, same object, or same semantic category.

## 7. Jump and Rhythm

Visible rhythm uses:

- short details
- shape matches
- beat-aware cuts
- fast-slow contrast
- repeated shot-size patterns
- music section changes

Do not hard-cut every beat. The course explicitly warns against dead, mechanical card-point editing. Cut slightly before or after beats when it feels more alive.

Use fast cutting when:

- the action is fast
- the music accelerates
- the point is montage, impact, transformation, or ad energy

Use slow cutting when:

- trust, emotion, premium display, explanation, or consequence needs landing

## 8. Music Thinking

Music has jobs:

- pre-load emotion before image/speech enters
- express inner world or theme
- create atmosphere
- structure sections
- lift a climax
- land an ending

Music search starts from:

- project type
- user/client need
- source material
- script tone
- speaker voice
- time/place/style keywords

For speech, prefer instrumental or low-distraction music. Duck music under speech. For a serious warning or trust-heavy business口播, a restrained bed is usually better than emotional over-scoring.

## 9. B-Roll Thinking

B-roll is not decoration. It should do one of these jobs:

- prove the spoken claim
- show product/process/place
- compress an abstract explanation
- bridge a visual jump
- reset attention after several face shots
- make a key word concrete
- carry a music-led section
- hide an unavoidable A-roll cut

If the script says a concrete noun, number, product, place, operation, result, or risk, scan for B-roll before deciding to keep face only.

## 10. The Finished-Video Gate

A publishable edit requires:

- content locked
- cut points cleaned
- visual/B-roll plan executed or deliberately rejected
- music/silence decision executed
- captions readable
- layout inspected
- render verified

If any gate is missing, state the real stage. Do not protect the agent's pride by naming a half-product "finished".
